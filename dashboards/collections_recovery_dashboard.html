Decomposition — why the claim doesn't hold up

1. It is a single-month comparison, not a trend

Of the six available month-pairs (Feb–Jul), MoM change was +11.0% only once (Feb→Mar). The other five pairs were −9.1%, −7.3%, +5.2%, −4.7%, +6.7%. Presenting the one positive outlier as "the" trend is a cherry-picked reference period, not a representative summary.

Fact

2. There is no statistically detectable trend

Regressing monthly recovery against month index gives R² = 0.004 — month explains essentially none of the variance. A flat line (no growth, no decline) fits the data about as well as any trend line would.

Fact

3. First-half vs second-half is flat

Jan–Mar average monthly recovery: ₹18.21 Cr. May–Jul average: ₹18.24 Cr. Change: +0.14% — not 11%, and not distinguishable from noise given the ±8.4-point month-to-month volatility.

Fact

4. Mix-adjustment doesn't change the picture

Risk-segment share of recovered value is stable within a 24.0–26.9% band every month (see Driver tab) — there is no meaningful portfolio-mix shift to control for. The "adjusted" and "independent" figures are therefore the same: +0.3%, not +11%.

Findings and business impact

Fact
Duplicate successful payments. 346 payment_ids appear twice, both copies marked SUCCESS — ₹2.59 Cr of recovery value would be double-counted if not deduplicated. Treatment: keep first occurrence only; applied throughout this dashboard.
Fact
Payment ↔ borrower mismatches. 2,078 payment records (846 unique borrower_ids) don't match the borrowers master; 2,034 of those conflict with the account-level borrower_id rather than being fixable by a simple join. 1,448 are SUCCESS payments worth ~₹10.85 Cr. Treatment: flagged, not silently overwritten — borrower-level cuts of this dashboard exclude these records.
Fact
Outstanding > principal. 13,029 of 30,000 accounts (43%) show outstanding_amount greater than principal_amount. No business rule was provided to explain this (fees/penalties vs. a data error) — retained and flagged, not corrected.
Fact
Borrower identity conflicts. 27,503 of 30,000 borrower rows belong to a borrower_id with conflicting name/phone/email/city/state across records — borrower_id alone is not a reliable identity key in this dataset.
Strong evidence
Agent/vendor identity is time-varying. All 1,000 agent_ids show conflicting employee_code, vendor_id, team or status across their 30 historical rows on average; 4,078 agent records (13.6%) have joined_at later than updated_at. Agent-level and vendor-level attribution requires an effective-dated join, not a flat lookup — not implemented in the current account_month table, which is a known limitation of this dashboard's agent cuts.
Strong evidence
Campaign attribution is ambiguous. 56.7% of targeted accounts touched by 2+ campaigns; "latest campaign" attribution is materially different from a windowed attribution (see Driver tab).
Correlation
Disposition taxonomy changed mid-period. call_dispositions.disposition_code contains both "PROMISE_TO_PAY" and "PTP" as distinct values alongside a disposition_version field — consistent with a vendor/schema code change rather than two different outcomes. Not yet reconciled into a single taxonomy.
Fact
Timestamp anomalies. 30,191 account_status_history records have recorded_at earlier than event_at (backdated/late-arriving events). 1,827 calls (2%) have no agent_id. 2,400 call_attempts (2%) have no vendor_id. 158 call_ids have conflicting duplicate rows (same ID, different data).

A

Where should the ₹10 Cr go?

Headline answer: the data cannot reliably choose one option today

Two things are true simultaneously in this dataset. First, no cost, spend, salary, or vendor-pricing field exists anywhere in the 17 source tables — ROI, cost-per-₹-recovered, and break-even are therefore not computable for any of the six options, only assumable. Second, none of the operational levers tested (channel, agent tenure, DPD, targeting priority) shows a statistically meaningful effect on recovery in the current data — the "why" behind the Feb→Mar spike remains unexplained. Committing ₹10 Cr against either an unproven cost model or an unproven driver is a bet, not a data-backed decision.

C

Directional lean (not a recommendation)

If forced to choose today

Fix targeting/attribution logic first — it is the one lever with a demonstrated, large, and controllable effect on reported numbers (7x swing in matched payments across attribution windows) and is far cheaper than ₹10 Cr. It de-risks every other decision, since campaign and channel performance cannot be trusted until attribution is fixed.

Before committing the ₹10 Cr

Run a controlled pilot (e.g. randomized channel or staffing allocation across a matched set of accounts) for 2–3 months with cost tracked from day one. That converts today's "no evidence of a driver" into either a real, measurable ROI case or a documented reason to not invest in that lever.

D

What data/experiment is needed to de-risk this decision

  • Cost data: agent fully-loaded cost, telephony per-minute/per-call cost, WhatsApp/SMS per-message cost, field-visit per-visit cost, AI voice vendor pricing.
  • A single, documented attribution rule (fixed window + tie-break) applied consistently, so channel/campaign comparisons stop moving with window choice.
  • An effective-dated agent↔vendor↔team mapping, so agent/vendor performance isn't computed on a flat (and currently 100%-conflicting) lookup.
  • A true randomized or quasi-experimental test (e.g. difference-in-differences around a real strategy change, or an A/B test) — the mid-year "targeting strategy change" referenced in the assignment brief was not detectable in this data (no break in targeting volume, priority, or channel mix was found), so no counterfactual estimate could be produced without fabricating one.
